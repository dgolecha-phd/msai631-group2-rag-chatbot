# Concept: Correlation and Causation
Correlation means two variables move together. Causation means a change in one variable produces a change in the other. A correlation alone does not prove causation.
Two variables can be correlated because of a third variable that affects both. Ice cream sales and swimming accidents rise together in summer because of hot weather, not because one causes the other.
To support a causal claim, researchers use controlled experiments, where people are assigned to groups at random, or careful study designs that rule out other explanations. In your writing, use words such as "is associated with" unless you have that kind of evidence.
