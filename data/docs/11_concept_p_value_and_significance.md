# Concept: Statistical Significance and the p-value
A p-value is the probability of seeing a result at least as extreme as the one observed if there were really no effect. A small p-value, commonly below 0.05, is taken as evidence against the idea of no effect.
A p-value does not tell you how big or important an effect is. With a very large sample, a tiny and unimportant difference can still be statistically significant.
